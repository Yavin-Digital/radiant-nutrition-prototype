What Does a Registered Dietitian Actually Do?

Jenny Brewton, RD Apr 2026 5 min read

If you've ever Googled "nutritionist near me" and felt confused by the results, you're not alone. The terms nutritionist and registered dietitian are used interchangeably all the time — but they are not the same thing, and that difference matters when it comes to your health.

Nutritionist vs. Registered Dietitian: What's the Difference?

In most states, including Texas, anyone can legally call themselves a "nutritionist." There is no required degree, no licensing exam, and no oversight. A personal trainer, a wellness blogger, or someone who completed a weekend certification can all use that title.

A Registered Dietitian Nutritionist (RDN) or Registered Dietitian (RD) is a different story entirely. To earn that credential, a person must:

  • Complete a bachelor's or master's degree in dietetics or nutrition from an accredited university
  • Finish a supervised practice program (similar to a medical residency) totaling at least 1,000 hours
  • Pass a national board examination administered by the Commission on Dietetic Registration
  • Maintain continuing education credits every five years to stay current

In Texas, RDs must also hold a state license as a Licensed Dietitian (LD). Every dietitian at Radiant Nutrition is a credentialed RD or RDN — not just a nutritionist.

Quick rule of thumb: Look for the letters RD, RDN, or LD after a practitioner's name. Those credentials mean they've met rigorous national and state standards — not just attended a seminar.

So What Does an RD Actually Do at an Appointment?

A lot more than hand you a food list. Here's what a typical first visit at Radiant Nutrition looks like:

  1. Comprehensive intake: We review your health history, current medications, labs, lifestyle habits, and food preferences — the full picture.
  2. Goal setting: Together we identify what you actually want to achieve, whether that's managing a diagnosis, losing weight, improving athletic performance, or simply feeling better.
  3. Personalized nutrition plan: We build a plan around your real life — not a generic template. Your schedule, budget, food preferences, and cultural background all shape the plan.
  4. Ongoing support: Follow-up visits track your progress, troubleshoot challenges, and adjust the plan as your life and goals evolve.

Who Should See a Registered Dietitian?

The short answer: almost everyone can benefit. But RDs are especially valuable if you're dealing with any of the following:

  • Type 1 or Type 2 diabetes, or prediabetes
  • Heart disease, high cholesterol, or high blood pressure
  • Weight management goals (loss or gain)
  • Digestive issues like IBS, Crohn's, or celiac disease
  • Eating disorders or a complicated relationship with food
  • Sports performance and body composition goals
  • Pediatric nutrition concerns
  • Pregnancy or postpartum nutrition
  • General wellness — wanting to eat better without a specific diagnosis

Is It Covered by Insurance?

Often, yes. Medical nutrition therapy (MNT) with a registered dietitian is a covered benefit under many insurance plans — especially for conditions like diabetes, kidney disease, and eating disorders. At Radiant Nutrition, we're in-network with several major plans, and we verify your benefits before your first visit so there are no surprises.

Even if your plan doesn't cover nutrition visits, our self-pay rates are transparent and our dietitians offer packages designed to fit a variety of budgets.

Jenny's Tip: Call your insurance company and ask: "Do I have a benefit for Medical Nutrition Therapy (MNT) with a Registered Dietitian?" Many people are surprised to find they've had this benefit all along and never used it.

Why It Matters to Work with a Credentialed Professional

Nutrition advice is everywhere — social media, podcasts, the internet — and much of it is conflicting or flat-out wrong. An RD cuts through the noise with evidence-based guidance tailored specifically to you. We don't sell supplements, follow trends, or push a one-size-fits-all protocol. We practice medical nutrition therapy grounded in science and adjusted to your individual biology and lifestyle.

If you've tried diets that didn't work, felt overwhelmed by conflicting advice, or are managing a health condition that's affected by what you eat — a registered dietitian is exactly the kind of partner you need.
